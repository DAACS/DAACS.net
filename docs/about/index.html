About

DAACS is a suite of technological and social support to optimize student learning. It provides personalized feedback about students’ strengths and weaknesses in terms of key academic (reading, writing, and mathematics) and self-regulated learning skills, linking them to resources to help them be successful students. DAACS is an open source project and institutions are free to use and adapt DAACS for the specific needs of their students. Please contact us for more information.

DAACS was developed under grants #P116F150077 and #R305A210269 from the U.S. Department of Education. However, the contents do not necessarily represent the policy of the U.S. Department of Education, and you should not assume endorsement by the Federal Government.

Why DAACS? Why Now?

DAACS is an effective, low-cost approach to boosting students’ college readiness.

Diagnostic assessments of both academic (i.e. reading, writing, and mathematics) and non-academic (i.e. self-regulated learning; metacognition, motivation, and learning strategies) knowledge and skills provides a comprehensive picture of students’ abilities. The feedback from the assessments and the accompanying links to open educational resources (OERs) enable students to boost their skills on their own, while also enabling their institutions to provide targeted interventions based upon individual student needs.

  • In rigorous research, students in the DAACS treatment group achieved a higher average GPA (+0.13, p < 0.01) during their first semester compared to students in the control group. This is equivalent to about a half a letter grade.

  • Students who complete the assessments and also review the feedback have higher GPAs and retention rates than those who do not.

  • The inclusion of DAACS data in predictive models increases their accuracy by at least 2% and as much as 11%.

DAACS has a higher correlation with freshman GPA than the SAT/ACT.

Components of the DAACS intervention

DAACS has four major components: 1) untimed, no-stakes, online diagnostic assessments of students’ readiness for college in terms of self-regulated learning, reading, writing, and mathematics; 2) feedback on the assessments that includes recommended strategies and links to OERs to help students address deficiencies; 3) predictive models that identify students at risk, as well as the specific risk factors; and 4) information that enables instructors and academic advisors to help students build on strengths and address deficiencies identified by the assessments.

The Assessments, Feedback, and OERs

DAACS focuses on four domains related to college readiness:

  • Self-regulated learning: This survey collects information about students’ metacognition, learning strategies, and motivation. Instant feedback includes links to the DAACS SRL Lab

  • Writing: The writing assessment serves as both a reflection on students’ SRL results and feedback, and an assessment of their writing skills. This assessment has students summarize the results and feedback from their SRL survey, identify SRL skills they will use as a college student, and commit to using them. Their writing is assessed in terms of nine criteria. Essays are machine scored using theLightSide software that provides feedback within one minute, along with links to the Excelsior’s Online Writing Lab

  • Reading: The reading assessment provides feedback on students’ comprehension skills, including structure, inference, language, purpose and ideas, with links to the Reading Comprehension Lab.

  • Mathematics: The mathematics assessment is adaptive. Students receive feedback on geometry, lines and functions, numbers and calculations, statistics, variables and equations, and word problems, up to and including precalc, with links to OERs such as Math is Fun

How does DAACS work?

DAACS is not meant to place students nor to measure their growth.

Completing all four DAACS assessment typically takes 90-120 minutes, often as part of a first-year experience course or college orientation.
